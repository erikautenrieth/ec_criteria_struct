Inclusion Criteria:
Present to ED primary for management of LBP, defined as pain originating between the lower border of the scapulae [AND] and the upper gluteal folds. Flank pain, that is pain originating from tissues lateral to the paraspinal muscles, will [NOT] not be included.
Musculoskeletal etiology of low back. Patients with [NOT] non-musculoskeletal etiologies such as urinary tract infection, [OR] ovarian cysts, [OR] or influenza like illness will be [NOT] excluded. The primary clinical diagnosis, at the conclusion of the ED visit, must be a diagnosis consistent with [NOT] non-traumatic, [NOT] non-radicular, musculoskeletal LBP.
Patient is to be discharged home. Patients admitted to the hospital are more likely to be treated with parenteral medication and therefore are [NOT] not appropriate for this study.
Age 18-64 Enrollment will be limited to adults younger than 65 years because of the increased risk of adverse medication effects in the elderly.
 [NOT] Non-radicular pain. Patients will be [NOT] excluded if the pain radiates below the gluteal folds in a radicular pattern.
Pain duration <2 weeks (336 hours). Patients with more than two weeks of pain are at increased risk of poor pain [AND] and functional outcomes.(9)
Prior to the acute attack of LBP, back pain [NOT] cannot occur more frequently than once per month. Patients with more frequent back pain are at increased risk of poor pain [AND] and functional outcomes.(9)
 [NOT] Non-traumatic LBP: [NOT] no substantial [AND] and direct trauma to the back within the previous month
Functionally impairing back pain: A baseline score of > 5 on the Roland-Morris Disability Questionnaire

Exclusion Criteria:
 [NOT] Not available for follow-up
Pregnant [OR] or breast-feeding
Chronic pain syndrome defined as use of any analgesic medication on a daily [OR] or near-daily basis
Allergic to [OR] or intolerant of investigational medications
Contra-indications to non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs: 1) history of hypersensitivity to NSAIDs [OR] or aspirin 2) active [OR] or history of peptic ulcer disease, chronic dyspepsia, [OR] or active [OR] or history of gastrointestinal bleed 3) Severe heart failure (NYHA 2 or worse) 4) hypertension (JNC7 stage 2 or worse) 5) Chronic kidney disease 3 or worse 6) Current use of anti-coagulants 7) Hepatitis 8) Alcoholism
Contra-indications to muscle relaxants: 1) Concurrent use of centrally acting opioids; 2) Renal impairment; 3) Liver abnormality including cirrhosis [OR] or elevated enzymes 4) Use of any of the following medications: fluvoxamine, [OR] fluoroquinolones, [OR] amiodarone, [OR] mexiletine, [OR] propafenone, [OR] verapamil, [OR] cimetidine, [OR] famotidine, [OR] acyclovir, [OR] ticlopidine, [OR] oral contraceptive pills
