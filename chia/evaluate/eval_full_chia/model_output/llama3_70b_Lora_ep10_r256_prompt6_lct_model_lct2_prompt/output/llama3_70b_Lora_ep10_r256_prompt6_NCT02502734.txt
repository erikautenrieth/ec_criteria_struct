Inclusion Criteria:
Aged 5 years to less than 12 years at Visit 1. At least 15 (25%) children of the total study population must be aged 5 to less than 8 years. 
Male [OR] or pre-menarchial female subjects. 
Subjects must be pre-adolescent [AND] without any signs of puberty (Tanner Stage 1). 
Normal range for their height [AND] and weight. Weight [AND] and height measurements should fall within the percentile range 3-97% of normal values for age according to Danish growth charts. 
Have a documented diagnosis of persistent asthma, as defined by the National Institutes of Health for at least 3 months prior to the Screening Visit. 
A pre-bronchodilatory forced expiratory flow in 1 second (FEV1) at Visit 1 (Screening) >=80% predicted. There should be [NOT] no Short acting beta-agonist (SABA) use within 4 hours of this measurement. 
Using one of the following asthma therapies prior to entry into the study: SABA inhaler alone (e.g. salbutamol) on an as required basis [OR] and/or Regular [NOT] non-inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) controller medications for asthma (e.g. cromones [OR] or leukotriene receptor antagonists) [OR] and/or Previously treated with ICS (equipotent to inhaled budesonide <=400 micrograms (mcg) total daily dose). There must be [NOT] no ICS use within 2 weeks of Visit 1 (Screening). 
Able to replace their current SABA treatment with study supplied rescue SABA provided at Visit 1 for use as needed for the duration of the study. 
Written informed consent from at least one parent/care giver (legal guardian) and accompanying informed assent from the subject (where the subject is able to provide assent) prior to admission to the study: (1) If applicable, subject must be able and willing to give assent to take part in the study according to the local requirement. The study investigator is accountable for determining a child's capacity to assent to participation in a research study, taking into consideration any standards set by the responsible independent ethics committee (IEC). (2) Subject and their legal guardian(s) understand that the study requires them to be treated on an outpatient basis. (3) Subject and their legal guardian(s) understand that they must comply with study medication and study assessments including recording of peak expiratory flow [AND] and rescue SABA use, attending scheduled study visits, and being accessible by a telephone call. 

Exclusion Criteria:
A history of life-threatening asthma defined for this protocol as an asthma episode that required intubation, [OR] hypercapnea requiring [NOT] non-invasive ventilatory support, respiratory arrest, [OR] hypoxic seizures [OR] or asthma-related syncopal episode(s). 
Subjects with a history of asthma exacerbation requiring the use of systemic corticosteroids (tablets, [OR] suspension, [OR] or injection) for at least 3 days [OR] or a depot corticosteroid injection [OR] or emergency room attendance (within 3 months) [OR] or requiring hospitalization for asthma (within 6 months) prior to screening. 
Significant, [NOT] non-reversible active pulmonary disease (e.g. cystic fibrosis, [OR] bronchiectasis, [OR] tuberculosis). 
Culture-documented [OR] or suspected bacterial [OR] or viral infection of the upper [OR] or lower respiratory tract, [OR] sinus [OR] or middle ear that is [NOT] not resolved within 4 weeks of Visit 1 and led to a change in asthma management [OR] or, in the opinion of the Investigator, is expected to affect the subject's asthma status [OR] or the subject's ability to participate in the study. 
Any fracture in the leg to be measured within 6 months prior to the screening visit. 
Any metabolic disorders [OR] or other diseases that may impact on normal growth patterns. 
 [NOT] No major surgery requiring general anaesthesia for at least 3 months prior to the screening visit. 
 [NOT] No febrile illnesses [AND] with temperature >39 degree celsius for more than five consecutive days within the week preceding the Screening Visit. 
Any significant abnormality [OR] or medical condition identified at the screening medical assessment (including serious psychological disorder) that in the Investigator's opinion, [NOT] preclude entry into the study due to risk to the subject [OR] or that may interfere with the outcome of the study. 
Clinical visual evidence of candidiasis at Visit 1 (Screening). 
Use of any of the prohibited medications listed in protocol. 
Strenuous physical exercise within 3 hours of Visit 1 (Screening) 
Drug allergies: Any adverse reaction including immediate [OR] or delayed hypersensitivity to any intranasal, [OR] inhaled, [OR] or systemic corticosteroid therapy. Known [OR] or suspected sensitivity to the constituents of the ELLIPTA Inhaler (i.e., lactose, [OR] FF). 
Milk Protein Allergy: History of severe milk protein allergy. 
The subject has participated in a clinical trial [AND] and has received an investigational product within the following time period prior to the first dosing day in the current study: 30 days, 5 half-lives [OR] or twice the duration of the biological effect of the investigational product (whichever is longer). 
Exposure to more than 4 investigational medicinal products within 12 months prior to the first dosing day. 
[NOT] Unable to use the ELLIPTA inhaler [AND] and peak flow meter correctly. 
An affiliation with the Investigator site: the parents/guardians [OR] or child is an immediate family member of the participating Investigator, sub-Investigator, study coordinator, [OR] or employee of the participating Investigator. 
The Parent or Guardian has a history of psychiatric disease, [OR] intellectual deficiency, [OR] substance abuse [OR] or other condition (e.g. [NOT] inability to read, comprehend or write) which may affect: validity of consent to participate in the study; adequate supervision of the subject during the study; compliance of subject with study medication [AND] and study procedures (e.g. completion of daily diary, attending scheduled clinic visits); subject safety [AND] and well-being. 
Children in care: Children [AND] who are wards of the government [OR] or state are [NOT] not eligible for participation in this study.
